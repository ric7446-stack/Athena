1.0	 Introduction: 
1.1 General Introduction
This marketing plan describes the strategic plan for the brand extension of the established Colombian coffee company, Café Quindío S.A.S., into Canada's specialty retail market. This plan speaks to the broader consumer trend of "premiumization" in the at-home coffee segment for consumers, allowing Café Quindío to potentially secure a share of the growth of that segment. 
Founded in 1991 by Nubia Motta Camargo, Café Quindío has been a leading Colombian coffee roaster for over 30 years (Café Quindío, 2024). The company was built on a "farm-to-cup" philosophy that emphasizes social responsibility within the coffee culture of the company's region of origin. In addition to having a strong production capacity, the company has an incredible depth of experience in brand experience because they operate 33 of Café Quindío's own café-restaurants, or coffee house-style restaurants, throughout Colombia. Café Quindío's dual skill set in production and retail, as well as demonstrated experience exporting coffee to the United States, Europe, and Middle Eastern markets, uniquely positions Café Quindío as a strong candidate for brand extension.

The strategy outlined here is to launch a multi-tiered product portfolio through the brand's premium offerings. This product launch set is organized in a way that will appeal to specific segments of the premium market simultaneously: 
First, we have the core line, which goes by the name "Familia Gourmet" (whole bean and ground), which will be the economically priced, premium product.
Second, we see the Ultra-Premium Line called "Kit Cosechas Municipales" (i.e., Salento, Circasia, etc) targeting the connoisseur market and premium gifts.
Third and last, the innovation line, a sub-portfolio of "Orgánico," "Descafeinado," "Honey," and "Vanilla," is directed to specialized niches and differentiated from the competition.
This portfolio will be launched in a phased manner, prioritizing selected urban markets in Toronto, Ontario, and Vancouver, British Columbia, that have profitable, higher-income opportunities.
1.2 Marketing Context
This marketing plan is justified by a clear and delineated opportunity in the Canadian market. The Canadian coffee market is large and growing significantly, with a total retail value of CAD 2.9 billion in 2024 (Euromonitor International, 2025a). This value represents 7% current terms growth over the prior year, and a forecast compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 5% through 2029 (Euromonitor International, 2025b).
The opportunity for Café Quindío within the Canadian coffee market can be found in the primary driver of this growth—a definitive shift in consumer behavior. Canadian consumers are "increasingly migrating away from instant coffee towards fresh coffee options", influenced by a "rising interest in a premium at-home coffee experience" (Euromonitor International, 2025a). This shift in consumer behavior is most prominent among Generation Z and Millennial consumers, who identify fresh coffee as "higher quality and better tasting". The Canadian retail market is evolving to support coffee purchasing trends as well, with physical retail stores practicing "experiential retail"; where stores engage in-store customers through events and community-oriented activities to create customer loyalty (Euromonitor International, 2025c).
There is also a strong targetable demographic connection. Canada has a growing Latin American population, which tripled in the 25 years leading to the 2021 census (Statistics Canada, 2024b). 
Finally, the economic and political environment is advantageous. Toronto (2.76M) and Vancouver (2.60M), which are our target launch markets, are two of Canada's wealthiest cities with median household income that can support a premium-priced portfolio (Statistics Canada, 2024c; Statistics Canada, 2024d). The Canada Colombia Free Trade Agreement (CCOFTA) provides favorable import conditions, including the elimination of tariffs for Colombian coffee, and stable, rule-based trading conditions (Global Affairs Canada, 2022)
1.3 Marketing Objectives
To leverage the context detailed above, the following are four specific, measurable, and time-bound (SMART) objectives this plan will pursue:
Within the first 18 months of operations, the objective is to achieve strategic distribution for the "Familia Gourmet" and "Líneas de Innovación" portfolios in 15 key accounts in premium retail (e.g. Whole Foods, Loblaws, Pusateri's) in the Toronto and Vancouver markets.
The goal is to establish the "Kit Cosechas Municipales" as a premium gift/connoisseur offering and sell 500 units within the first year through specialty retail and direct-to-consumer online sales. 
Aiming to capture 1% share of the Canadian fresh coffee market by the end of Year 3, thereby establishing an initial footprint versus key premium competitors like Kicking Horse Coffee (4.7% share) (Euromonitor International, 2025a). thus, establishing Café Quindío as a recognized/authentic "lifestyle brand" in the Canadian "foodie" and ethical consumer segments as measured by a 5% engagement rate on an Instagram channel dedicated to Canada.
1.4 Strategic Focus
The key premise of this marketing plan is that Café Quindío will be differentiated from all other products on the coffee market because we will sell, not another coffee product but an authentic, holistic, and ethical Colombian coffee experience.
Unlike our key premium competitor, Kicking Horse Coffee, which positions its brand primarily on "organic" and "fair trade" certifications (Euromonitor International, 2025d), our strategic focus is on "Origin and Diversity."
This value proposition will be delivered through our multi-faceted portfolio. The "Familia Gourmet" provides genuinely sourced origin at a premium accessible level; the "Líneas de Innovación" (Honey, Vanilla, Orgánico) and "Kit Cosechas Municipales" (Salento, Circasia) provide diversity of taste and discovery that no single competitor can replicate. Use of Café Quindío's 30-year history and 33 store retail legacy is central to selling not just a product, but the rich, diverse, and authentic coffee culture of the Quindío region originally centered on the story of its founder, Nubia Motta.

1.5 Expected Results
This marketing plan is designed to deliver measurable outcomes across multiple dimensions. Within the first 18 months, Café Quindío expects to establish a strong retail presence through strategic partnerships with 15 premium retailers in Toronto and Vancouver. The "Kit Cosechas Municipales" line is projected to generate CAD $15,000 in revenue from 500 units sold during Year 1. By the end of Year 3, the company aims to capture 1% market share of the Canadian fresh coffee market, representing approximately CAD $29 million in retail value. Additionally, brand awareness metrics will be tracked through social media engagement, targeting a 5% engagement rate on the dedicated Canadian Instagram channel, indicating successful penetration of the "Ethical Foodie" and "Nostalgic Diasporan" target segments. These results will position Café Quindío as a recognized premium Colombian coffee brand in Canada, establishing a foundation for future expansion.

2.0 Mission & Vision
2.1 Mission
Our goal is to connect the Canadian market to the authentic quality and rich cultural heritage of Café Quindío. We will accomplish this by launching a multi-tiered portfolio centered around the "Familia Gourmet"; "Líneas de Innovación"; and "Kit Cosechas Municipales" which will target both the discerning Canadian consumer as well as the established Latin American community. The overall strategy includes adjusting the presentation of the portfolio for the bilingual (English/French) Canadian retail framework, defining selective distribution exclusively to premium distribution channels, and establishing "Origin and Diversity" as a critical value proposition.

2.2 Vision
Café Quindío envisions becoming the leading authentic Colombian coffee brand in Canada, recognized for delivering an unparalleled coffee experience that celebrates the rich cultural heritage and exceptional quality of the Quindío region. Within five years, the company aims to be the preferred choice for discerning Canadian consumers seeking premium, ethically sourced coffee that tells a genuine story of origin. Through continuous innovation, community engagement, and unwavering commitment to quality, Café Quindío will establish itself as a lifestyle brand that transcends the product itself, creating lasting connections between Canadian consumers and Colombian coffee culture.

3.1 Internal Analysis
3.1.1 Company Strengths
It is important to emphasize that Café Quindío's first internal strength is the authenticity of its brand story, supported by extensive operating experience. Established in 1991, Café Quindío has 30 years of experience, with a clear "farm-to-cup" philosophy based on social responsibility (Café Quindío, 2024). This authenticity is verifiable, representing an inherent differentiator for consumers desirous of "migrating" toward "premium at-home coffee experiences" (Euromonitor International, 2025a). 
The company is also an experienced retailer, evident in its operation of 33 physical café-restaurants throughout Colombia. This experience demonstrates the company's deep institutional knowledge of how to create a "lifestyle experience," which readily supports the contextual identification of "experiential retail" in Canada (Euromonitor International, 2025c). 
Finally, the experience is bolstered by a multi-tiered, diverse product portfolio. This internal strength affords Café Quindío the option to launch a complex assortment (Gourmet, Kits, Innovation) in a sophisticated, multi-segment targeting strategy without being prematurely distracted.
3.1.2 Weaknesses
Conversely, it is vital to have a sober assessment of the internal weaknesses that might hinder the company's performance, from the tactical to the strategic level. The first and foremost internal weakness is that our 'country of origin' is a double-edged sword. While Colombian origin represents a key advantage, the average Canadian consumer may not distinguish between "premium Colombian coffee" and generic Colombian coffee that has been sold as a commodity for decades. This places the burden of proof on Café Quindío to invest in marketing efforts that educate consumers and differentiate the brand from commodity perceptions. Unlike Café Quindío, Kicking Horse Coffee benefits from a "premium" Canadian country of origin and does not face this challenge.
 This challenge is further complicated by the substantial gap in financial and market power. Café Quindío is an independent family-founded company, which is entering a market to compete with brands that are owned by global conglomerates. Our principal competitor, for example, Kicking Horse Coffee, is owned by Lavazza Group, and the overall market is dominated by giants like Kraft (19.4% share) and Nestlé (13.5% share) (Euromonitor International, 2025a). This internal financial weakness fundamentally limits our performance by making a traditional, high-spend advertising campaign impossible.
Lastly, tactically, the company's current product offering is out of compliance with the law in Canada (Canadian Food Inspection Agency, 2025a), which is a hurdle in terms of time and money in operational matters that can be resolved.

For Opportunity (Trade), the major political opportunity is the Canada-Colombia Free Trade Agreement (CCOFTA), which has been in effect since 2011, and led to a more stable rules-based trading regime. Importantly for this plan, the agreement eliminated tariffs on 98% of products including Colombian coffee, creating a more competitive price point for our products, as the price to import will be lowered (Global Affairs Canada, 2022). 
Though there are certain threats or hurdles (Regulation). The biggest legal hurdle is the need to comply with regulations. The Canadian Food Inspection Agency (CFIA) has strict guidelines regulating all "consumer prepackaged" food (Canadian Food Inspection Agency, 2025b). The challenging aspect related to regulations, for the current product line, lies within the requirement that all mandatory information, including both common name and net quantity, to be displayed in a bilingual format of both English and French (Canadian Food Inspection Agency, 2025a). Additionally, compliance will require a "Nutrition Facts table" to accompany the product labels (Canadian Food Inspection Agency, 2025c).

3.2.4 Five Forces Model.
Threat of New Entrants
Emerging brands represent a High Threat of New Entrants in the premium coffee market in Canada. While the challenges we face as an international brand looking to enter the Canadian market are significant (CFIA, high listing fees from retailers, logistics), new domestic competitors face little postponement. It is common for local cafés, entrepreneurs, or microbatch coffee roasters to use their own brands. These micro-roasters compete at the same premium grocery shelf and target the same premium consumers, thereby increasing competition.
Bargaining Power of Buyers
Buyers (Canadian consumers) have an elevated level of bargaining power. This power is the result of two factors: diminishing switching costs and a high concentration of alternative offerings. A consumer can easily "taste" a new coffee brand with little financial or functional risk. Price sensitivity supports this power also; even in the premium segment, consumers can easily compare our product against Kicking Horse, Starbucks (competitors), and similar products (aka private label). This high-level power indicates the pricing strategy will need to be precise, and the brand story compelling enough to create loyalty.
Threat of Substitute Products or Services
At this point, it should be considered that the threat of substitutes is High. 
Direct Product Substitutes: There are quite a few categories of substitutes, including various other hot drinks, with the large Canadian tea market being especially noteworthy. 
Indirect Product Substitutes: This category includes all other formats of coffee drinks including instant coffee and coffee pods, as well as the mass-market "non-premium" ground coffee that dominates the coffee market in Canada. 
Service Substitutes: This represents a significant portion of the overall threat, as the large "out-of-home" coffee market (e.g., Tim Hortons, Starbucks, or other coffee shops) that compete against the "premium at-home experience" (Euromonitor International, 2025a).
Bargaining Power of Suppliers
This force represents a significant internal Strength for Café Quindío. The bargaining power of suppliers is Low. This is because our company is not a simple reseller; it is a 30-year-old roaster with an integrated "farm-to-cup" philosophy (Café Quindío, 2024). The 'suppliers' of our core raw material are part of our established business model. This gives us immense control over quality, cost, and, most importantly, the authenticity of our brand story, which is a key differentiator from competitors who may rely on third-party green bean brokers.
Rivalry Among Existing Competitors
Rivalry is intense. While our plan avoids the mass-market giants (e.g., Keurig, 27.1% share) (Euromonitor International, 2025a), the premium-niche sector is an intense battleground. Our primary competitor, Kicking Horse Coffee, is well-entrenched with a 4.7% market share (Euromonitor International, 2025).

Our focus strategically is solely on premium-fresh subsector worth over CAD 1.4 billion (Euromonitor International, 2025a). It is relevant to characterize the main competitors Café Quindío will encounter.

Starbucks (Whole Bean): Represents the "mass-premium" competitors. Starbucks' strength lies primarily in its strong brand recognition. Our product will be directly competing on-shelf with their "Familia Gourmet" and "Líneas de Innovación".

4.0 STPD (Segmentation, Targeting, Positioning, Differentiation)

Building upon the situational analysis, this section defines the strategic approach to market segmentation, target audience selection, brand positioning, and competitive differentiation.
To successfully establish Café Quindío within the new Canadian market, it is imperative to understand who we are trying to reach, how to engage them, and what sets us apart. This STPD framework is a critical element that synthesizes the findings from our Situational Analysis (Part 3) to build the foundation for all subsequent Marketing Mix decisions. This analysis will allow us to define the different groups of consumers who might be interested in a premium Colombian coffee and focus on which groups we want to target. Knowing who we are reaching will help us position Café Quindío in a way that resonates with them based on its unique Colombian identity.
4.1 Segmentation
The Canadian coffee market, valued at CAD 2.9 billion (Euromonitor International, 2025a), is highly heterogeneous. Thus, to properly establish which marketing approaches to take, we need to segment this broad market into smaller, more meaningful groups. Our segmentation will take a layered approach that utilizes our external analysis.
The first segmentation variable is psychographic, which segments the market by consumer values. Our analysis identifies an important and growing segment of consumers who are "migrating" to "premium at-home coffee experiences" (Euromonitor International, 2025a) and actively care about ethically and sustainably sourced coffee (Consumer Lifestyles in Canada, 2025). This group of consumers stands apart from a mass-market consumer segment in which consumers are price and convenience-focused (e.g., pods and instant coffee).
Within this psychographic segmentation will be a demographic refinement. The premiumization trend appears to be primarily derived from Generation Z and Millennials (Euromonitor International, 2025a), who also have the high disposable income in our target cities of Toronto and Vancouver (Statistics Canada, 2024c; 2024d) to consider a premium portfolio. There is also a separate demographic segmentation opportunity, with the 71,080 Colombian-born residents (Statistics Canada, 2024b) as a "beachhead market" niche, and then the broader Latin American community as a more converged market.
Lastly, as our competitive analysis showed (Section 3.2.4), there is also a behavioral segmentation at play that is focused on price sensitivity which shows three clearly defined tiers in the market: Mass-Premium (e.g., Starbucks, ~$14.99), Premium (e.g., Kicking Horse, ~$15.99), and Ultra-Premium (e.g., Pilot Coffee, ~$22.25+). Our diverse portfolio (S3) is distinctly situated to deal with all three of these premium behavioral segments. 
4.2 Targeting
Considering the segmentation data and our multi-tiered portfolio, the best approach is to target 2 primary groups to achieve our goals, achieving both mass-market appeal and cultural authenticity. 
The first target is the "Ethical Foodie" (Broad Market). This consumer segment is adventurous, socially active, and follows the "premiumization" trend (Euromonitor International, 2025a). Demographically, high-income Millennials or Gen Z in Toronto or Vancouver (Statistics Canada, 2024c; 2024d) comprise this segment. The unmet need of the "Ethical Foodie" is a brand that matches the authenticity of a "third wave" roaster like Pilot but offers the access and flavor diversity that Kicking Horse (predominantly organic) does not provide (Euromonitor International, 2025d). This group will be reached via our dual-channel approach: utilizing our D2C e-commerce (Section 3.2.3), and our "experiential retail" strategies in premium grocers (Euromonitor International, 2025c).

4.4 Differentiation
Café Quindío's uniqueness in the Canadian market stems from the authenticity of source and emotional connection built through our portfolio. This uniqueness will justify our premium price point in a "High Rivalry" market (Section 3.2.4).
The first aspect of uniqueness is our "Blue Ocean" product portfolio. Unlike our competitors who are bound to one value proposition (e.g. "organic"), our portfolio represents a "one-stop-shop" for Colombian coffee culture. Our core benefit is unique in that we can deliver a premium core (Gourmet), an ultra-premium gift (Kits), and more innovative flavors (Honey, Vainilla) all under the umbrella of one trusted brand.
The second aspect is our verifiable brand story as it is difficult to imitate. As identified in our VRIO analysis (Section 3.1.3), our "from farm to cup" supply chain (S1) is a Sustained Competitive Advantage. It enables us to provide a strong emotional and lifestyle value. For "Ethical Foodies" (Target A), it is the value of authenticity and ethical sourcing. For "Nostalgic Diasporans" (Target B), it is the strong emotional value associated with home. Our story, as it is our core benefit, distinguishes us from the competition.
